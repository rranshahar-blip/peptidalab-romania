Bacteriostatic water for laboratory use: composition, records and limits

What to check on the label and in batch records, without reconstitution or administration instructions

Published and updated: 27 August 2026 · Author: PeptidaLab editorial team

Bacteriostatic water is not a peptide. The term is used for certain aqueous solutions that may contain an antimicrobial preservative, commonly benzyl alcohol. Exact composition must not be assumed from the name.

What a laboratory should verify

The label and batch dossier should identify composition, preservative concentration, volume, batch, expiry, storage conditions, sterilisation method and relevant testing.

Why no reconstitution instructions are provided

Compatibility depends on the material, method and experimental purpose. Generic instructions may compromise a sample or be misread as guidance for human use.

Status and handling

A container labelled “bacteriostatic” is not automatically authorised for every application. Supplier specifications and legal assessment define permitted use.

Method, sources and limitations

This material was written originally and reviewed to distinguish preclinical findings, human studies and commercial status. For legislation and authorisation, consult Directive 2001/83/EC, the EMA and relevant primary scientific literature.
